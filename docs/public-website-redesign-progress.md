## Approach

Since all 39 pages already share the same component classes (`.page-header`, `.highlight-box`, `.content-section`, `.sec-title`, `.tag-blue`, `.contact-form`, etc. — defined in `css/style.css`, `css/pages.css`, `css/services.css`, `css/home.css`), the base visual language is elevated **once, centrally, in those shared stylesheets** — which every page inherits automatically. This is the "one design system, not 39 different designs" approach the brief explicitly asks for, and is verified per-page (not assumed) via the Playwright checks in this table.

New shared components being added this pass (none existed before): accordion, tabs, breadcrumb, capability/proof block. These are added once to the shared CSS/JS and then adopted page-by-page where they genuinely improve understanding — not force-fit everywhere.

Pages needing real **content** restructuring (not just visual inheritance) beyond the shared CSS: Home (recommended narrative flow), Services (category reorganization), Solutions (problem→solution navigation), About (premium company narrative). These get individual attention; the rest inherit the elevated shared design system directly.

## Redesign notes (significant findings/decisions, most recent first)

### A third fabrication layer — modal "guarantee-item" overclaims on the 3 main hub pages

A final sitewide grep sweep (after all individual pages were believed complete) turned up a third, distinct fabrication layer that earlier passes had missed: the per-service/per-solution/per-industry **modal "OUR GUARANTEE" lists** on `services.html`, `solutions.html`, and `industries.html` — the site's 3 highest-traffic hub pages — still contained 24 overclaim lines across their ~21 modals, even though the `.modal-stats` blocks and one certification line on these same pages had already been fixed earlier this session. Patterns found: formal-sounding compliance claims ("PCI DSS Compliant", "HIPAA Compliant", "SOC 2 Compliance Path", "GDPR, HIPAA, and SOC 2 compliant data handling", "SOX, GDPR, HIPAA compliance built into the platform"), specific unverifiable SLA/uptime numbers ("99.99% uptime SLA", "99.95% uptime with failover"), specific unverifiable scale claims ("From 100 to 100,000 users", "From 10 to 10,000 vehicles", "Tested on 50+ device models"), and a specific response-time SLA promise ("Critical defects fixed within 24 hours"). Fixed all 24 with a literal-string-replacement script (11 on services.html, 6 on solutions.html, 7 on industries.html), softening each to honest capability/practice language consistent with every other fix this session (e.g. "PCI DSS Compliant" → "PCI DSS-Aligned", "99.99% uptime SLA" → "Built for demanding uptime requirements"). Verified: modal HTML structure intact (48/36/24 `.guarantee-item` elements respectively, matching pre-fix counts), one modal opened and its guarantee text inspected live, 0 console errors, 0 overflow on all 3 pages.

**Confirmed false positive during the same sweep:** `js/journey-render.js` (used only by `product-journey.html`, the placeholder/pending-approval page) also matched the `stat-number` pattern, but inspection showed it renders real computed values (milestone number, status, actual counts of deliverables/technical achievements from the page's own data) — not fabricated performance claims. No fix needed there.

**Takeaway:** a targeted fix (remove the obvious `.modal-stats` block) does not guarantee a page is fabrication-clean — a full-page, full-text sweep is needed even after a page has already been "fixed" once, since related overclaims can live in adjacent, differently-named markup the first pass didn't target.

A follow-up sitewide grep (broadened to catch `99.9x% uptime`, `fixed within 24 hours`, `From X to Y users/vehicles`, `50+ device models`, and remaining compliance-certification phrasing across *all* 39 pages, not just the 3 hub pages) caught 5 more individual instances of the identical pattern that had survived on 4 of the already-"done" individual service pages: `ai-solutions.html` ("GDPR, HIPAA, and SOC 2 compliant data handling" → "built to GDPR-, HIPAA-, and SOC 2-aligned standards"), `enterprise-solutions.html` ("99.95% uptime SLA" + "100 to 100,000 users" → non-numeric language), `cloud-solutions.html` ("99.99% uptime architecture" → "Built for demanding uptime requirements"), `defect-resolution.html` ("Critical defects fixed within 24 hours" → "prioritized and fixed quickly"). All 4 re-verified live (0 console errors, 0 overflow). A subsequent broadened sweep for award/trust-signal fabrication patterns ("award-winning", "trusted by N", "Fortune 500", "as seen in", etc.) returned zero matches sitewide.

### Sitewide "PROVEN RESULTS" fabrication + a hidden JS-injected fabrication bug — found and fixed

While live-verifying `web-development.html` (screenshot showed unexplained blank space below the fold), found two related, previously-undetected fabrication sources that the earlier static-HTML `grep` sweeps had missed entirely:

1. **17 static "PROVEN RESULTS" blocks** — a `<section>` on each of the 8 service pages, `saas-development.html`, `enterprise-solutions.html`, `ecommerce-development.html`, and all 6 industry pages, presenting invented numeric "results" as fact (e.g. "93% Prediction Accuracy", "47% More Sales", "99.99% Uptime", "4.8★ Average App Store Rating"). Removed all 17 blocks in one pass (verified via `grep -l "PROVEN RESULTS" *.html` returning zero files) and confirmed `<section>`/`</section>` tag balance stayed even in every file afterward.

2. **A hidden, JS-injected fabrication source**: `js/service-enhancements.js` — loaded on all 8 individual service pages plus `saas-development.html` and `enterprise-solutions.html` — dynamically injects a "Business Benefits You Can Measure" section at runtime with more invented metrics (e.g. "40% Faster Time to Market", "3x More Organic Traffic", "93% Prediction Accuracy", "1000+ Concurrent Users") that never appear in the static HTML source, so they were invisible to every grep-based fabrication sweep so far. This is the same violation as the removed `.modal-stats`/`PROVEN RESULTS` blocks, just delivered via JS instead of markup. **Fix:** removed the `benefits` data arrays (10 of them, one per page), the `buildBenefits()` function, and its insertion call from `js/service-enhancements.js`, keeping the legitimate `rootCause` ("Why X Projects Fail" — real consulting problem-framing, no invented stats) and `related` (real internal cross-links) sections intact. Bumped the script's cache-busting query param from `?v=1` to `?v=2` on all 10 pages that load it, since the local static server was serving a stale cached copy of the old script even after the file was edited on disk — confirmed via a direct `fetch()` diff between the cache-busted and non-busted URL before finding the real cause.

All 10 affected pages re-verified live after the fix: 0 console errors, 0 layout overflow, correct section order (Challenge → Root Cause → Solution → Guarantee → Explore More → CTA), 0 broken links/images in network requests. This finding is a good example of why the mandated live-render verification (not just static-file review) is required — a purely textual audit of the HTML source would never have caught it.

**Lesson applied going forward:** for every remaining page, check `document.body.innerHTML` at runtime (not just the saved `.html` file) for stat-like patterns before marking a page fabrication-clean, since shared JS can inject content invisibly to a source-only review.

Applying that lesson immediately turned up a second instance of the identical bug: **`js/industry-enhancements.js`** (loaded on all 6 industry pages — Healthcare, Finance & FinTech, Logistics & Transport, Education & EdTech, Real Estate, Retail & E-Commerce) injects the same kind of fabricated "Business Impact" metrics (e.g. "40% Reduced Wait Times", "100% Compliance", "35% Revenue Increase", "3x Lead Conversion"). Fixed identically — removed the 6 `benefits` data arrays, the `buildBenefits()` function, and its insertion call, keeping `rootCause` and `related`; bumped `?v=1` → `?v=2` on all 6 pages. Also found and softened several static-HTML "OUR GUARANTEE" overclaims on these same pages that read as formal certifications AskABD cannot substantiate (e.g. "HIPAA Compliant" → "HIPAA-Aligned", "PCI DSS Compliant" → "PCI DSS-Aligned", "99.99% uptime SLA" → "High-Availability Architecture", "Regular penetration testing" → "Security-First Engineering", "From 10 to 10,000 vehicles" → non-numeric scale language), consistent with the earlier About-page fix.

### Services page — real fabrication found and fixed
`services.html`'s 12 per-service detail modals each opened with a `.modal-stats` strip of 4 specific numeric "results" presented as fact — e.g. "Zero Breaches", "93% Prediction Accuracy", "10K+ Requests/Sec", "1000+ Concurrent Users" — with no real evidence behind any of them. One "Our Guarantees" item went further, claiming specific staff certifications ("CISSP, CEH, and OSCP certified professionals") with no basis to confirm they're real. **Fix:** removed all 12 `.modal-stats` blocks entirely (not replaced with softer numbers — removed, since no real figures exist to put there) and reworded the one specific-certification claim into an honest capability statement. The surrounding "Common Challenges" / "What We Deliver" / "Our Guarantees" (business-policy-style) content was left in place — it reads as real, non-fabricated positioning copy, not fabricated performance data.

Also restructured the page from a flat 12-card grid into the requested 8 category tabs (Business & Analysis, Software Development, Quality, Data, Digital Transformation, Smart/AI, Cloud & Infrastructure, Post-Delivery), reusing the real, already-existing per-service modal content rather than duplicating it — every category maps only to capabilities AskABD's existing pages already substantiate (no new services invented). "Cyber Security & DevSecOps" — flagged in Phase 1 as having no dedicated page — was placed under Cloud & Infrastructure since none of the 8 requested categories name security explicitly; still recommend a dedicated security page as a follow-up (Phase 1 finding, still open).
